# 10 · Presence — Agents That Feel Like People (Urge-Driven, Not Turn-Based)

## Thesis

Most multi-agent systems schedule behavior like queue-servers: every agent gets its turn, in round-robin, forever. The result reads like a conference-call recording — nobody ever lurks, nobody ever interrupts, nobody ever goes quiet because they're upset, nobody ever *misreads the silence*. The belief in the simulation is preserved only if the behavior is generated by the *social system*, not the scheduler.

**Presence is the research program for urge-driven agent behavior** — replacing turn-based scheduling with attention, interpretation, motivation, emotion, pressure, inhibition, and memory, so that multi-agent systems exhibit the properties that make social systems real: uneven noticing, late replies, lurking, masking, private alliances, misreading silence, and emergent social drama. It is the direct continuation of the Perfectman work (and its writeup, *When AI Agents Stop Taking Turns*).

## Why it matters

- **Credible simulations need credible actors.** The value of simulation (05) and of every multi-agent product — social platforms, market simulations, community moderation sandboxes, negotiation practice, synthetic-user panels — depends on whether the agents' *social behavior* is believable, and turn-based scheduling is the #1 believability killer.
- **The turn-based default is a lazy architecture.** It conflates "time" with "speaking", and it produces the known failure mode of multi-agent sims: everyone is always "on", everything is a broadcast, and the *absence* of a response carries no information. Real social systems run on pressure and inhibition — the *absence* is a signal.
- **It's the frontier of agent expressiveness.** The market has solved "agents do tasks." The open problem is "agents *feel*" — and the product surface (gaming, sims, social, synthetic users, virtual companions) is enormous.

## Core concept

### Social presence over ticks

Ticks can exist as backend polling or snapshot boundaries — but the *product behavior* should come from the agent's internal model of the social world:

- **Attention** — the agent notices what it attends to, not everything.
- **Interpretation** — the same message means different things to different agents.
- **Motivation** — why the agent is in the room; goals beat queues.
- **Emotion** — the state that changes thresholds (Russell's circumplex, in the Perfectman model).
- **Pressure & inhibition** — the mechanism that makes *silence meaningful*: an agent that is pressured to respond but inhibited from responding *is* producing behavior (the absence).
- **Memory** — the social history that accumulates and changes future behavior (Zep-style temporal memory in Perfectman).

### The initiative engine

The architecture that emerged from Perfectman: **initiative paths**, not turns. An agent's next action is driven by *urgency* (how much this matters to its motivation right now) modulated by *inhibition* (social risk, fear of misread, established norms). The engine decides when an agent *moves* — and, critically, when it *doesn't*, and that the *not* is legible to other agents.

### Cold-start and stagnation detection

Communities die when the conversation stalls. Presence must handle: cold-start bootstrapping (getting the room moving), stagnation detection (the room is stuck and *why*), and breaking mechanisms (what re-ignites a dying conversation). The Perfectman model has these explicitly; the research is to make them principles, not hacks.

## What gets built

1. **The presence engine** — reference implementation (Perfectman's `presence` core): urge/attention/emotion/pressure/inhibition computation per agent, the initiative engine, no global scheduler.
2. **The four-layer emotion model** — Russell's circumplex-based state, personality mutation, thresholds that shift with history — extracted from Perfectman's architecture docs into a reusable library.
3. **Believability probes** — measurement instruments: human-judged "does this feel like a room of people?" plus *behavioral* probes (interruption rate, lurking rate, response latency distribution, silence-misreading rate, alliance formation over time).
4. **The simulation harness** — a reusable environment (socket-chat-shaped or forum-shaped) for running presence-based agent populations, including the spectator stance (you can watch, which requires the sim to be legible).
5. **Safety layer** — the social-simulation equivalent of guardrails: personas, moderation, and the "no-one wants a room full of angry bots" controls. This is a *property of the harness*, not a bolt-on.

## Architecture sketch

```
agent
  ├─ attention → what it notices (per-agent salience)
  ├─ interpretation → meaning of what it noticed
  ├─ motivation → its goals (drives initiative)
  ├─ emotion → Russell circumplex state (drives thresholds)
  ├─ pressure/inhibition → when to act, when to stay silent
  └─ memory (temporal) → social history, alliances, grudges
           │
           ▼
     initiative engine (NO round-robin; urgency-driven moves)
           │
           ▼
     events (posts, replies, lurk, unmask, alliance) → social log
           │
           ▼
     believability probes + spectator view (safety, moderation)
```

## Research program

- **R1: The absence signal.** Can we measure, and then make legible, the information carried by *not responding*? (Latency distributions, silence-as-negotiation, the misreading-of-silence failure that Perfectman's notes call out.)
- **R2: Emergent structure without scripts.** Under pure pressure/inhibition mechanics, do the known social phenomena actually emerge *without being written* — alliance formation, scapegoating, norm enforcement, topic drift? The research is honest: *which* of these need explicit drives and *which* are true emergents?
- **R3: Calibration to human baselines.** When run against human room-mates, what are the distributional gaps (interruption timing, response latency, reply length, topic persistence)? This is the SimBench (05) discipline applied to social behavior.
- **R4: Persona transfer.** Do presence dynamics survive swapping personas? (The same engine, different people → different rooms.) This tests whether presence is *architecture* or *persona-writing*.
- **R5: Stagnation mechanics.** Under what conditions do rooms die, and what breaking mechanisms revive them *without looking scripted*?

## Risks / failure modes

- **Emergence theater.** Hand-rolled phenomena dressed as emergence — the research must *pre-commit* to which phenomena are claims of emergence (see R2) and detect scripted-ness with the probes.
- **The uncanny room.** Agents that are "too social" — high interruption, emotional volatility — read as chaos rather than presence. The pressure/inhibition axis is the tuning dial, and its calibration is research, not taste.
- **The moderation burden.** Emergent toxicity. The safety layer must be as first-class as the presence engine, not added when a room goes bad.
- **No objective evaluation.** Believability is slippery — that's what the believability probes (behavioral + human-judged) exist to make measurable.

## Success signals

- A room of presence-engine agents where an outside observer can watch a full social arc — someone lurking, someone misreading silence, an alliance forming — *without the script having written any of it*.
- Believability probes published with baseline numbers against turn-based scheduling (the "before" is easy to gather; the delta is the claim).
- A reusable `presence` library that another simulation (05's swarm sim) can plug in to replace its turn scheduler, with a measured improvement in social-behavior realism.
